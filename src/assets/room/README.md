# Interior photographs — drop them in here

Two slots in the "The room" section. Drop a file in, it appears; delete it,
the labelled frame comes back.

| File to add      | Slot              | Shape       |
| ---------------- | ----------------- | ----------- |
| `wide.webp`      | Room — wide       | 4:3 (wide)  |
| `counter.webp`   | Counter — detail  | 4:3         |

`.webp`, `.png`, `.jpg`, `.avif` all work. **Restart the dev server after
adding a brand-new file** — Vite reads this folder at start-up. Overwriting an
existing file hot-reloads normally.

---

## These two must be real photographs

This is the one place on the site where that is not negotiable.

The product pictures can be generic — a cup of coffee looks like a cup of
coffee, and nobody is misled. **These are different.** The section is headed
"The room" and the copy invites someone to come and see it. A generated or
stock interior here is a direct claim about a physical place a visitor is
being asked to travel to, and it would be false. Someone would walk in and
find a different room.

So: a phone photo of the actual room beats a beautiful picture of someone
else's. Leave the frames empty until there is a real one — an empty frame
says "no photo yet", which is true, and the paragraph beside it already makes
the argument without a picture.

**Do not generate these with an AI tool. Do not use stock.**

---

## Taking them

Nothing here needs a camera — a recent phone is fine.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Aspect** | 4:3 landscape, both of them. Anything else gets cropped to fit. |
| **Size** | 2000 px on the long edge is plenty. Under ~500 kB each; re-encode to WebP q80. |
| **Light** | Daylight, blinds open, every artificial light off. Overcast midday is the easiest. |
| **Flash** | Never. It flattens the room and turns the colour green. |
| **People** | Nobody identifiable in frame unless they have said yes. Empty is also fine — an empty room reads as calm here, not as deserted. |
| **Tidy first** | Cables, bins, stacked crates, staff bags, anything taped to a wall. The picture is the first impression. |

**`wide`** — stand in a corner, hold the phone at seated eye height rather than
standing height, and get the seating and the depth of the room in. Keep the
verticals straight; a tilted doorframe is the thing that makes an interior
photo look amateur. Do not use the phone's ultra-wide lens — it bends the walls
and makes the room look bigger than it is, which is its own kind of false.

**`counter`** — a close one. The counter edge, the machine, a stack of cups,
whatever the room actually looks like up close. Shallow depth of field if the
phone offers it.

## It does not need to match the product images

The product pictures are deliberately stripped back to white. These are
photographs of a real place and should look like it — real colour, real
light, real wood and walls. The contrast is the point: the product panels are
the idea, this is the thing itself.

Keep them a little quiet, though. If the camera has pushed the colour hard,
pull the saturation back slightly and lift the shadows a touch, so they sit
next to the rest of the page rather than shouting over it.
